# WHO Healthy Diet Fact Sheet

Source: https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/healthy-diet
Retrieved: 2026-09-09

## Key facts

- Diet plays a critical role in shaping the health and well-being of both individuals and populations, and unhealthy diets are a major risk factor for disease and disability.
- Healthy diets help to protect against malnutrition in all its forms, as well as noncommunicable diseases (NCDs), including diabetes, heart disease, stroke and cancer.
- Healthy dietary practices start early in life – breastfeeding fosters healthy growth and improves cognitive development.
- Dietary behaviours and preferences established in childhood and adolescence often extend into adulthood.
- Healthy diets come in many forms, but the four core principles of adequacy, balance, moderation and diversity should be the foundation of any healthy diet.
- A diet must also be safe to be healthy, i.e. free from microbial and chemical contaminants.
- A variety of minimally processed and unprocessed foods low in unhealthy fats, free sugars and sodium are the foundation of any healthy diet.

## Overview

Consuming a healthy diet throughout the life-course helps to prevent malnutrition in all its forms as well as a range of noncommunicable diseases (NCDs) and conditions.

Basic principles of a healthy diet:
- **adequacy**: meets, without exceeding, micronutrient and macronutrient needs such that deficiencies are prevented;
- **balance**: total energy intake is balanced with energy expenditure, with an adequate balance across the three primary sources of energy, protein, fats and carbohydrates;
- **moderation**: limited intake of nutrients, ingredients, and foods that may be detrimental to health;
- **diversity**: inclusion of a wide variety of nutritious foods within and across food groups.

## Carbohydrates

- Carbohydrates should represent approximately 45–75% of total daily energy for most people.
- Should come primarily from whole grains, vegetables, fruits and pulses (unprocessed maize, millet, oats, wheat, brown rice, lentils, chickpeas, beans, dried peas).
- Fresh, frozen, or canned fruit/vegetables are fine if no added sugar/excess sodium.
- Everyone older than 10 should aim for at least 400 g fruits and vegetables/day (250 g for ages 2–5, 350 g for ages 6–9).
- Daily dietary fibre target: at least 25 g for >10 years old (15 g for 2–5 years, 21 g for 6–9 years).

## Sugars

- Free sugars should be limited to <10% of total daily energy (≈50 g / 12 tsp for a 2000 kcal diet); ≤5% may provide additional benefits.
- Free sugars = added sugars by manufacturer/cook/consumer + sugars naturally in honey, syrups, fruit juices/concentrates.
- Reducing sugar intake should be done without substituting non-sugar sweeteners (acesulfame K, aspartame, sucralose, stevia, etc.).

## Fats

- Minimum 15% of daily energy from fat (adults); up to 30% or more depending on context.
- Total fat should be ≤30% of total daily energy to help prevent unhealthy weight gain in adults.
- Unsaturated fat (fish, avocado, nuts, sunflower/soybean/canola/olive oil) preferred over saturated fat (fatty meat, butter, palm/coconut oil, cream, cheese, ghee, lard) and trans fat.
- Saturated fat ≤10% of total energy; trans fat ≤1% of total energy. Industrially-produced trans fat should be avoided entirely.
- Reduce fat by: steaming/boiling instead of frying; using polyunsaturated oils instead of butter/lard/ghee; reduced-fat dairy and lean meats; limiting baked/fried/pre-packaged snacks.

## Protein

- 10–15% of total daily energy is generally sufficient for adults (≈50–75 g for a 2000 kcal diet).
- May be higher (>15%) for adolescents, athletes, bodybuilders.
- Excess protein can burden the kidneys.
- Mix of animal and plant sources; plant-based shift may reduce NCD risk in some contexts.

## Salt/sodium and potassium

- High sodium intake → increased blood pressure → cardiovascular disease risk. Potassium mitigates this effect.
- Main sources: processed foods (ready meals, processed meats, cheese, salty snacks), bread, and salt added during cooking/at table (bouillon, soy sauce, fish sauce, table salt).
- Adult limit: <5 g salt/day (2 g sodium/day). Lower for children (based on energy intake).
- Reduce by: limiting salt/high-sodium condiments in cooking, no salt/sauce at table, limiting salty snacks, choosing lower-sodium products.
- Potassium target: ≥90 mmol/day (3510 mg/day) for adults, from fresh fruit/vegetables.
- Lower-sodium salt substitutes (LSSS) can help reduce sodium and increase potassium, but caution for hyperkalaemia risk (e.g. CKD patients — relevant for NCD chatbot's CKD-safe filtering logic).

## Vitamins and minerals (micronutrients)

- ~30 essential micronutrients (13 vitamins + 16 minerals: iron, iodine, zinc, calcium, etc.).
- Deficiencies → anaemia, scurvy, cognitive impairment, neural tube defects.
- Globally: >50% of children under 5 and >2/3 of non-pregnant women of reproductive age are deficient in ≥1 key micronutrient (iron, zinc, vitamin A, or folate most common).
- Encourage nutrient-dense foods: fruits, vegetables, whole grains, legumes, nuts, seeds, lean animal-source foods.

## Foods

- Diverse diet (fruits, vegetables, pulses, wholegrains, lean protein) → better micronutrient adequacy and lower NCD risk.
- Shift toward plant-based protein (especially away from red meat) may benefit many adults.
- Limit foods high in unhealthy fats, free sugars, sodium — especially highly processed foods.
